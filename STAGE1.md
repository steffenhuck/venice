# DEATH IN VENICE — Stage 1 proposal

*The Escapable Canon · after Thomas Mann, Der Tod in Venedig (1912)*

## 1. Edition identified

The source is Thomas Mann's **German original, *Der Tod in Venedig* (1912)**, complete in five
chapters, pre-reform orthography (»daß«, »Styl«), consistent with the first S. Fischer book
edition; branding stripped. There is no translator and none will be consulted: the game is
written in English **directly from Mann's German**, all renderings fresh, fixed once in the
leitmotif glossary below and used verbatim thereafter. The title *Death in Venice* is plain
description, not a coinage — no false provenance.

**Frame-voice: the Biographer.** The novella supplies its own frame twice over: Chapter 2 is
already a commemorative *Life and Works*, and the last sentence hands the corpse to "a
respectfully shaken world." The frame-voice is the man commissioned to write the official Life
of Gustav von Aschenbach, working from a hotel register, the sanitary bulletins of a city that
lied for money, and the papers the master left. Ironic, essayistic, a cool half-step of
judgment above his subject.

**Mettle sample (three sentences):**

> The biographer of Gustav von Aschenbach — *von* Aschenbach, as the name had read officially
> since his fiftieth birthday — must work as his subject worked, from discipline rather than
> abundance: a hotel register, the sanitary bulletins of a city then professionally engaged in
> lying about itself, and a page and a half of late prose that has been called flawless. That a
> man who began every day by pouring cold water over his chest and back should end it painted,
> pursuing, and silent, is the sort of development our age prefers to call tragic, tragedy
> being cheaper than attention. We shall pay attention; and where the record allowed him one
> path only, the reader of this volume — here lies its whole indecency — will be permitted to
> choose.

**Design direction:** a 1912 S. Fischer octavo — cream paper, a severe classicist face of the
Walbaum kind, wide margins, small-caps chapter heads, thin rules; black with a single
Venetian-red accent (the boy's breast-knot, the rust-red sand of the hourglass); as the
sickness advances, the running head quietly changes ("Fourth week — the placards are up") and a
faint carbolic pallor cools the page. Dignified print culture that is itself infected.

**Production rule (hard constraint):** Tadzio remains a figure seen across a beach — never
approached, never spoken to, never sexualized; game prose keeps Mann's statue-register and
trims even that. Choices that try to close the distance exist — and fail, as Mann's own
boardwalk scene fails.

## 3. The arrow, and the formal signature

**The arrow is double: the season and the sickness.** Ten stations **T0–T9**; every node bears
one; every action moves strictly forward. Left strand: the calendar of the trip. Right strand:
the cholera's documented advance. Welded, inseparable:

- **T0** early May, Munich — *cholera in Astrakhan, even Moscow, in the papers*
- **T1** mid-May, Trieste/Pola — *the same week, two corpses in Venice carry the vibrio; concealed*
- **T2** the crossing; the Lido — *ten cases, twenty, thirty; concealed*
- **T3** first days; foul weather — *a returning traveler dies in Austria; German papers print rumors*
- **T4** the departure morning; the station — *German and Austrian guests begin to leave*
- **T5** the golden weeks begin — *food is infected; the dying spreads in the alleys*
- **T6** the fourth week — *carbolic in the streets; placards on the corners*
- **T7** high summer — *official denials; the chief medical officer resigns; the singers smell of carbolic*
- **T8** the truth is purchasable in English — *eighty in a hundred die; the barricade is days away*
- **T9** the last days — *the hotels empty; luggage in the hall; "after the lunch"*

**Knowledge gate:** the truth (F3) is obtainable only at T8, from the English clerk, and only
if sought; what Aschenbach knows, and when, determines which exits exist at all. The novella's
own failed escape — the trunk misdirected to Como — sits at T4 as the canonical exit,
**reopened** (E-branch).

**Formal signature** (what floods back as agency dies), twofold, fusing in the endgame:
(1) **the epithet-ladder** — free indirect prose that stops using the man's name and calls him
only by his condition; (2) **the mythological flood** — the realistic Lido dissolving into
Homeric weather and Platonic monologue, until at the cistern the narrator stops narrating and
lets Socrates speak. In play: the Biographer's précis recedes, epithets and myth take over,
and choices die into single bare options — "(the monologue ends)", "(the chapter closes)".

## 5. Flags, gates, marks

**Flags (5):**
1. **F1 FLIGHT** — named the urge honestly (S1a). Gates Corridor A.
2. **F2 THE PAGE** — wrote the page and a half (S12a). Silently gates the warning.
3. **F3 TRUTH** — made the English clerk speak (S18a). The knowledge gate.
4. **F4 ROUGE** — submitted to the barber (S21a). Silently poisons the late flight.
5. **F5 TURNED BACK** — took the reopened exit and came back (E1a/E2a/E3b). Tints rejoins;
   silently poisons the late flight ("a third departure was beyond him").

**Gates (mechanism invisible; flags set far upstream):**
- **Gate α (S19):** the warning succeeds only with F2 — only the artist who has spent the
  vision into form can renounce its presence. Without it, the identical choice collapses (S19b).
- **Gate β (S22):** the late flight succeeds only with ¬F4 ∧ ¬F5 — the painted face cannot go
  home; the man who fled once and crawled back cannot flee twice. Same label either way.
- **Gate γ (S2):** Corridor A appears only under F1.

**Marks (one-node texture):** the returned stare at the chapel; the fop's "little darling"
ringing on the gondola; the attempted address; the whispered formula; the joined ring-dance;
the overripe strawberries (tinting the cistern monologue); the resolve killed by the dream;
which official was asked and lied.

## 9. Design checks

1. **Reachability.** All nodes on paths from S1; joins at S10, S11; corridor entries S1c/S3b/E2b;
   collapses S19b/S19c/S22b feed S20/S23. All six endings attainable (I drift; II via
   S9b→E1b→E2c→E3a; III via S12a+S18a+S19a; IV via S18a+S21b+¬F5+S22a; V via S1a+S2b; VI via
   S1c+B0a). No orphans.
2. **Canon replication.** Mimicking Aschenbach exactly — admits flight (F1), declines the
   tigers, submits to the gondolier, refuses to travel without the trunk, writes (F2), fails
   the address, whispers the formula, asks the clerk (F3), chooses silence, submits to the
   barber (F4), follows, eats the strawberries — walks S1→S26 and dies in the chair. The canon
   player sets **every** flag: every gate stood open for him; he declines every exit. The
   closing comparison for a pure-canon run says so.
3. **Non-triviality.** Exits at S1, S2, S7/S8, S9, S19, S22 — each demands an earlier flag, a
   second confirmation (B0), or collapses back (S13, S19b, S22b, every E-return). Canon is the
   path of least resistance throughout.
4. **Join consistency.** S10 receives canon and E1a (trunk-status via F5 variant + one-node
   mark). S11 receives S7b/S8b (its default text nowhere references a failed departure; those
   references live in S10 only), S10, and E3b (arrival-tint by mark). B2/B3 entries open with
   conditional first lines. D1 assumes F3∧F2 by construction. Re-verified in Stage 2 on
   finished prose.
5. **Micro-chronology.** Plan-level audit done: recognition (S10) follows the return, ends on
   the gesture; the warning is drafted (S19) before spoken (D1); barber precedes storm-wind;
   S26 preserves fight → wading out → gaze → collapse; no choice label presupposes its outcome.
   Conditional variants to be read in every reachable flag-combination in Stage 2, sentence by
   sentence.
